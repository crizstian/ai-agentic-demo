**Simplify and Focus**

Harness’ New Product Naming & Packaging Strategy

**Internal — Confidential**   ·   Jyoti Bansal, Founder and CEO   ·   August 10, 2026

# **Executive Summary**

Harness currently asks the field to sell 16 modules across software delivery, security, runtime protection, testing, reliability, and cost management. The breadth is valuable, but the customer-facing structure is too complex to sell and too fragmented to market.

We will **retire** **modules as the primary GTM, marketing, and packaging construct**. We are not retiring the underlying functionality.

Harness will take four **Agents** to market. **Each Agent is a complete product aligned to a clear buyer and outcome:**

1\.  **Harness Software Delivery Agent**

2\.  **Harness Security Testing Agent**

3\.  **Harness Runtime Protection Agent**

4\.  **Harness Cost Management Agent**

Together, these four Agents reposition Harness around the **Autonomous SDLC** while keeping the commercial model simple: customers activate only the capabilities they need within each Agent.

The four Agents are the customer-facing products GTM sells. All four share one unified Harness AI experience and one common AI foundation.

This achieves three goals:

•   **Simplification:** Four clear Agent stories replace 16 module stories.

•   **AI relevance:** Autonomous SDLC and the Agent names make our existing AI capabilities visible and move Harness beyond traditional CI/CD messaging.

•   **Focus:** The four Agents represent approximately **93% of projected new ACV this year**, and they get the field's full attention. The rest of the portfolio becomes our **Strategic Bets**, sold and grown inside the product rather than through the core sales motion.

Existing customer pricing remains unchanged.

# **3\. The Problems We Are Solving**

## **1\. Sixteen modules create too much GTM complexity**

The sales organization is expected to understand, position, and sell 16 separate modules, each with its own buyer, use case, competitive set, demonstration, pricing model, and proof points.

Sellers naturally default to the few modules they know best. ‘Ramp takes longer, deals require more specialist support, and customers hear a catalog instead of a clear Harness story.

## **2\. Our current structure makes Harness appear less AI-native than it is**

Most current module names describe categories that existed before the current AI shift. Adding AI to portfolio headings does not change the perception that Harness is a traditional software delivery company adding AI features.

Meanwhile, buyers increasingly want to discuss Autonomous SDLC, AI-driven delivery, agents, and autonomous software factories. New entrants such as Factory and Cognition are capturing that attention, even where they are not real competitors to Harness.

The problem is not a lack of AI substance. Harness already has Expert Agents, Worker Agents, built-in AI workflows, lifecycle context, governed execution, and verification. The module-based structure hides that reality and keeps us in a DevOps-tool conversation instead of a broader AI-transformation conversation.

## **3\. Some modules serve emerging personas and use cases**

Most of our revenue comes from a set of buyers and use cases we know well, and we have a proven motion for reaching them. Some modules serve buyers adjacent to those, or use cases that are still being fully defined in the market. Those are real opportunities, but they need a different way to reach customers.

Putting all of them in the core field motion consumes sales cycles, enablement time, and specialist attention without proportional output. Selling those through the core field works poorly in both directions. The rep is calling on a buyer they do not know with a use case the market has not settled on, and the product gets a fraction of the attention it needs to find its footing.

## **4\. Too many modules dilute marketing and company focus**

Every module creates pressure for its own positioning, launch, campaign, content, analyst coverage, pipeline target, and field attention. Marketing gets spread too thin, product launches compete with one another, and customers see a collection of tools rather than one coherent platform. Packaging discipline is therefore also organizational discipline.

# **4\. The Plan**

Four rules define the new model:

1\.  **Retire modules as the primary GTM construct.** The underlying functionality remains.

2\.  **Take four Agents to market.** They drive positioning, sales plays, enablement, campaigns, pipeline reporting, and portfolio investment.

3\.  **Keep capabilities modular.** Customers activate and pay for the capabilities they use within each product.

4\.  **Concentrate the core field on the four Agents.** The four Agents cover the buyers and use cases where our revenue is concentrated. The rest of the portfolio goes to market as Strategic Bets inside the product, run by a small Strategic Bets Growth team in the Product Org.

# **6\. Why the Products Are Named Agents**

# The Agent naming is designed to be immediately legible to the market. The industry has already embraced **coding agent** as the term for an AI system that performs software-development work rather than only suggesting code. Harness extends that familiar naming model to the downstream work required to safely ship and run software.

| Agent | Work it owns |
| :---- | :---- |
| **coding agent** | Creates and changes code |
| **Software Delivery Agent** | Takes each change safely through production |
| **Security Testing Agent** | Finds and fixes risk before production |
| **Runtime Protection Agent** | Protects software in production |
| **Cost Management Agent** | Governs and optimizes cloud and AI costs |

# This makes the names intuitive rather than invented. Buyers can immediately understand what each product does, while Agent signals that it can understand context and take action.

# The naming is also accurate today, not aspirational. All four products already combine Expert Agents, Worker Agents, built-in AI workflows, product capabilities, shared SDLC context, and governed execution.

# **7\. Why we can easily justify these Products Are Agents**

Calling these products Agents is accurate today, not a future-state claim. An Agent is a product with **context** about the environment, **intelligence** to reason over it, **execution** to act on it, and **governance** over every action. Every product already combines:

•   **Expert Reasoning** that understands, investigate, explain, recommend, create, and configure through natural language.

•   The **SDLC Knowledge Graph**, which provides shared context across repositories, services, teams, pipelines, deployments, security, runtime, costs, and outcomes.

•   **Worker Agents** and built-in AI workflows that perform substantive work, not only make recommendations.

•   Product capabilities that provide the tools through which work is performed.

The **Agent Harness** binds these elements together. Its Governed Orchestration Engine runs work through Harness Delegates inside customer environments and applies identity, permissions, policy, approvals, deterministic workflows, verification, evidence, rollback, and cost controls.

Together, these elements allow each Agent to reason over live context, recommend actions, perform work under policy, and verify the result.

Customers access all four Agents through one Harness AI experience across the UI, CLI, and IDE. Coding agents and other tools reach the same context and capabilities through Harness MCP.

**Risk-Based Autonomy**

Autonomous SDLC does not mean every task runs autonomously. Customers choose the right level of autonomy based on the task, environment, and risk:

Customers can start with the controls they use today and expand autonomy only where they are ready. The same identity, permissions, policies, approvals, verification, and evidence apply in every mode. Customers draw the lines, and Harness enforces them.

We will use **Worker Agents** as the canonical name, shortened from the public launch name **Autonomous Worker Agents**.

# **8\. GTM and Operating Implications**

The company talk track should stay simple: Harness is the platform for the Autonomous SDLC. We sell four Agents across software delivery, security testing, runtime protection, and cost management. Each combines AI reasoning with governed action, and customers activate only the capabilities they need.

| Function | What changes |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Sales and enablement** | Reps sell four Agents, not 16 modules. Each is a complete product with a clear primary buyer, outcome, discovery motion, and expansion path. |
| **Marketing** | Campaigns, events, content, and executive messaging concentrate on Autonomous SDLC and the four Agent stories. Capability launches strengthen the relevant product rather than create another top-level story. |
| **Product and portfolio** | Roadmaps focus on capabilities and cross-capability experiences that make each product stronger. A capability team does not automatically receive an independent broad-field motion. |
| **Pipeline and reporting** | Pipeline is created, forecast, and inspected primarily by product – the four Agents – with capability detail underneath.  |
| **Existing customers** | Existing modules become capabilities. Pricing, entitlements, commitments, and contracts remain unchanged. This transition does not require pre-Flex customers to move to Flex. |

Expert Agents, Worker Agents, the SDLC Knowledge Graph, and governed execution are the shared foundation across the four Agents. They are not additional products for the field to sell.

## **Guardrails**

•   Do not recreate 16 pseudo-products or independent field motions underneath the four Agents.

•   Do not reduce the **Agent** to chat. Each Agent must continue to combine reasoning, context, real action, governance, and verification.

•   Do not change pricing, entitlements, or bundle requirements as part of this transition.

•   Lead with the buyer problem and Agent outcome. Use the AI architecture to explain why Harness is differentiated—not as more terminology for sellers to memorize.

# **9\. Strategic Bets Growth Within the Product Org**

The following offerings will move to a focused **Strategic Bets Growth motion** organized within the **Product team**, outside the core sales motion:

• **AI SRE.** Detects, diagnoses, and helps resolve production incidents.  
• **AI Evals.** Tests and evaluates how AI applications and agents behave.  
• **Internal Developer Portal.** Service catalog, scorecards, and developer self-service.  
• **Feature Management & Experimentation.** Feature flags, progressive rollout, and experimentation.  
• **Resilience Testing.** Fault injection and resilience validation.

The decision to move these products to “Strategic Bets” status is based on two factors.  It is not a reflection of the quality or viability of products. 

1. These products serve personas who are organizationally adjacent to Harness’ core personas, and/or  
2. They support use cases that are still evolving in the market (Category is not yet fully defined.) 

For example, feature flag conversations typically lead us to the customer’s product organization which is adjacent to, but distinct from Harness's other core personas.  Similarly, AI SRE leads us to the Site Reliability Team, who are also distinct from our core personas.

Some of our Strategic Bets are changing quickly because of AI. For example, Feature Management is evolving toward “Runtime Configuration for AI applications”. And the Internal Developer Portal is evolving toward a collection of autonomous workflows and a catalog built for consumption by agents as well as humans.  Use cases and features are similarly evolving for other products in the Strategic Bets category.

Importantly, Strategic Bets run on the same platform as the four Agents, leveraging the same identity, policy, context, and Flex pricing meter. The principal change we are making is in how and when we engage customers.  We will engage customers at precisely the moment they need the capability- rather than waiting (hoping) for an AE to navigate the organization and engage the appropriate persona at the right time.  Strategic Bets will enable us to develop, experiment and refine our in-product selling motions.

**AI Test Automation:** AI Test Automation will not continue as a standalone product. We are finding that the primary use case people want is test execution through our CI pipelines, so it will become an additional meter within the Builds (CI) capability of the Software Delivery Agent. It is listed here so the full portfolio is covered in one place.

**How they go to market**

• **Growth happens inside the product.** Discovery, nudges, trials, activation, and expansion all happen inside Harness, designed and run by the Strategic Bets Growth team.  
• **Consumption runs on Flex.** Usage burns HSUs from the balance the customer already bought, under the same meter as every other capability. For a Flex customer, utilizing the capability is a product decision, not a purchase. Pre-Flex customers purchase under their existing terms. As with the rest of this strategy, nothing here requires a customer to move to Flex.  
• **Inbound demand has an owner.** The Strategic Bets Growth team handles inbound interest and acts as the specialist when a large opportunity shows up in an account. Strategic Bets Growth team will manage the opportunity, freeing up the sales team capacity.  
• **The core field does not carry them.** No plays, campaigns, launches, or enablement for Strategic Bets in the core motion. That capacity goes to the four Agents. Reps are still credited for pre-Flex revenue or post-Flex unit consumption from these products in their accounts.

**Existing customers and deals in motion are unaffected.** Customers using these products keep their entitlements, pricing, support, and roadmap commitments. Deals in flight close as scoped.

**Where this goes next**

Packaging will keep moving. Some Strategic Bets will stay product-led. Some will become capabilities inside one of the four Agents. Some could potentially become a fifth Agent we take to market. We will know more once we see how each one grows inside the product. For the rest of this year, the plan above is what we are running with. The Strategic Bets Growth motion is new for us, so we will work out the details as we go and expect to iterate.

# **10\. Naming Rationale**

## **Platform category: Autonomous SDLC**

| Option | Strength | Limitation |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **AI Software Delivery** — current | Established, clear, and consistent with current website positioning | Sounds like a traditional delivery platform with AI features; does not communicate autonomy |
| **AI-Native Software Delivery** | Signals that AI is built in rather than added later | Describes how the platform is built, not the future state it enables |
| **AI DLC** | Short and AI-forward | DLC is less familiar than SDLC and the meaning is ambiguous |
| **Agentic DLC** | Makes agents central to the story | Jargon-heavy, less durable, and can be confused with Agent DLC |
| **Autonomous Software Factory** | Vivid and ambitious | Can imply Harness owns the complete requirements-to-code system; fits software production better than runtime protection, cost management, and governance |
| **Autonomous SDLC** — selected | Makes a clear autonomy claim, spans all four Agents, and supports governed, progressive autonomy | Less vivid than software factory and requires a broad definition of the lifecycle |

**Decision:** Use **Autonomous SDLC** as the top-level category. It communicates a real change in how the lifecycle operates without requiring Harness to replace every coding, planning, or third-party agent.

At the website relaunch, **Autonomous SDLC** replaces **AI Software Delivery** as the top-level category. Software factory can remain useful as vision language, but not as the formal Harness category.

## **Product names: \<Function\> Agent**

The function name gives the buyer immediate clarity. **Agent** signals that the product can understand goals, reason over context, coordinate capabilities, and take action.

| Selected name | Why | Alternatives considered |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Software Delivery Agent** | Broad enough to cover code, builds, artifacts, infrastructure, databases, and deployments while preserving Harness's software delivery equity | Autonomous Delivery Agent can sound limited to CD and implies every action is autonomous |
| **Security Testing Agent** | Creates a clear before-production boundary with Runtime Protection and maps directly to the DevSecOps motion | Application Security Agent is broader but weakens the clean testing-versus-runtime distinction |
| **Runtime Protection Agent** | Precisely describes protecting applications, APIs, bots, and AI systems in production | Runtime Security Agent can imply host, workload, container, or broader CNAPP coverage |
| **Cost Management Agent** | Covers visibility, attribution, governance, budgeting, unit economics, and optimization | Cost Optimization Agent is too narrow; FinOps Agent is too tied to one persona |

Use the singular **Agent** because each name represents one complete product, even when it coordinates multiple Expert Agents and Worker Agents underneath.

On first external mention, use the full names: **Harness Software Delivery Agent**, **Harness Security Testing Agent**, **Harness Runtime Protection Agent**, and **Harness Cost Management Agent**.

We rejected traditional platform or suite names because they lose the AI signal, plural Agent names because they sound like usage units, and unique branded names because they recreate the complexity this strategy is intended to remove.
